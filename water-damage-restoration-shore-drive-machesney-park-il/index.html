Water damage restoration in the Shore Drive area of Machesney Park starts with one question: where did the water come from? Clean water from a failed pipe is handled differently from river water or groundwater, and the first safety steps change with the source.

This page covers the streets around Shore Drive in ZIP code 61115. It is written for homeowners and renters near the Rock River who want to know what to do when water gets in.

Flood water, groundwater and plumbing leaks are different jobs.

River water and floodwater can carry sewage, soil and chemicals. Standard dry-out methods for a burst supply line are not enough for that. Contaminated water usually means removing wet porous materials such as carpet padding and some drywall, then cleaning and drying what stays.

See flood damage cleanup for how river and groundwater losses are handled, and sewage backup cleanup when a drain or sewer line is involved. A clean plumbing leak can often be dried with the steps on the structural drying page.

  • Stay out of standing water that may touch outlets, cords or appliances.
  • Do not run fans, vacuums or other equipment in or near standing water.
  • Keep children and pets out of any water that may be contaminated.
  • Skip DIY drying for sewage or river water. Call for an assessment.

After the water recedes.

When the river drops, the work is only starting. Pumping standing water comes first, then removing damaged materials, then drying framing and subfloors with air movers and dehumidifiers. The water extraction page explains the pumping step, and basement water damage cleanup covers lower levels.

Moisture meters and thermal imaging help show where water went behind walls. Drying that stops too early can leave damp framing behind finished surfaces, and mold can follow. EPA guidance says to dry water-damaged materials within 24 to 48 hours where possible.

Insurance and flood coverage.

Standard homeowners insurance generally covers sudden water damage from a burst pipe or failed appliance. It generally excludes flood unless flood coverage was bought separately, and the policy decides what applies. Ask your insurer before work starts, and read your policy for sewer and sump backup wording.

Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope of work give your insurer the facts it asks for. Documentation does not guarantee payment. Your insurer decides coverage, and the restoration crew does not adjust claims.

Before the crew arrives near Shore Drive.

These steps are general and aim to keep people safe first. If anything feels unsafe, stay out and call.

1Stop the source if it is safe.

Close the nearest shutoff valve or the main water valve. If the water came from outside, do not wade into it.

2Keep clear of electrics.

Do not touch outlets, cords or the panel if water is near them. Ask your utility or an electrician about power before going back in.

3Photograph the damage.

Take photos and short videos of the water, the affected rooms and damaged belongings once it is safe to enter.

4Lift what you safely can.

Move small items, rugs and paperwork to dry ground. Leave heavy furniture and anything you cannot lift safely.
