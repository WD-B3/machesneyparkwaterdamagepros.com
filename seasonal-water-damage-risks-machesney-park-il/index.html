Seasonal Water Damage Risks to Check Every Year in Machesney Park, IL.

Each season brings a different water problem. A short yearly routine catches most of them.

By the Machesney Park Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-11-10

☎ Call (815) 314-9281.

Water damage in northern Illinois follows the calendar. Snowmelt and rain test basements in spring, storms test roofs in summer, leaves clog gutters in fall, and cold snaps test pipes in winter. This guide gives Machesney Park, IL homeowners a short checklist for each season.

None of the checks takes long, and they are cheaper than the repairs they prevent.

Spring: thaw, rain and sump pumps.

Spring brings snowmelt and heavy rain. Test your sump pump by pouring water into the pit and watching it run. Check that the discharge pipe is clear and directs water away from the house. Look at basement walls for new damp spots.

If the basement does flood, see the basement page.

Summer: storms and humidity.

Summer storms can drop heavy rain quickly and damage roofs. Walk the attic after a storm, check for stains and damp insulation, and look for loose shingles from the ground.

Run a dehumidifier in damp basements, and check the AC drain line for clogs. The AC leak page covers that cause.

Fall: gutters and shutoffs.

Clean gutters and downspouts so water does not spill at the foundation. Disconnect and drain garden hoses, and shut off outdoor spigots where there is an interior shutoff.

Check the water heater and its connections before the heating season starts.

Winter: freezes and ice.

Insulate exposed pipes in garages, crawl spaces and exterior walls. Let faucets drip on the coldest nights if pipes are at risk, and know where the main shutoff is. Keep heat on when you are away.

The frozen pipe page explains what to do if a pipe freezes or bursts.

Check the shutoff and supply lines yearly.

Once a year, turn each shutoff valve to be sure it moves, and replace old rubber hoses on washers and dishwashers with braided stainless connectors. Locate and test the main valve.

See appliance leak water damage for common failure points.

The thaw and refreeze cycle.

Late winter often swings above and below freezing. Snow melts, water runs off roofs, and then it freezes again at the eaves. Ice dams form and can push water under shingles into attics and walls.

Keep gutters clear, and if ice builds up, look for ceiling stains below. The ceiling water damage page explains what those stains can mean.

Small habits that prevent big losses.

Know where your main valve is, keep a flashlight and bucket by the basement stairs, store valuables off the floor in lower levels, and put a water alarm near the water heater and sump pit. These cost little and warn you early.

When you leave for a trip in winter, keep the heat on and ask someone to check the house.

A calendar you can copy.

Put four reminders on your phone. In March, test the sump pump. In June, check the attic and AC drain. In October, clear gutters and drain hoses. In December, check pipe insulation and confirm the main shutoff.

Each reminder takes under half an hour. Together they cover the most common seasonal causes of water damage.

Storm night checklist.

When a heavy storm is forecast, clear storm drains near your property, move valuables off basement floors, check that the sump pump is plugged in and running, and keep a flashlight handy. After the storm, walk the attic and basement.

If water gets in, stop the source if it is safe and call. The 24/7 line answers during storms.

Machesney Park note: the Rock River and stormwater.

The Village of Machesney Park stormwater page says rainwater in the village runs directly into the Rock River, and the village operates a municipal separate storm sewer system under an Illinois EPA permit. The Village of Machesney Park 2040 Comprehensive Plan reports that more than 58 percent of village homes were built before 1980 and that a significant portion of the village lies in the 100-year floodplain of the Rock River.

Keep storm drains and ditches near your property clear of debris, and check the FEMA map for your address.
