Kitchen Water Damage Restoration in Machesney Park, IL.

Cabinets, toe kicks and subfloors hide water. Here is how kitchen leaks are found and dried.

☎ Call (815) 314-9281.

Kitchen water damage restoration in Machesney Park, IL deals with water that escapes from a sink drain, a supply line under the sink, a faucet or the cabinet area around them. Kitchens pack plumbing, appliances and wood cabinets into a small space, so a small leak can ruin a lot of material.

If cabinets are swelling or the floor feels soft near the sink, shut the angle stop valves under the sink and call. If the leak came from a dishwasher or refrigerator line, the appliance leak page covers the appliance side.

What a leaking sink base looks like.

The cabinet under the sink is the usual place to find kitchen water damage. Look for a swollen or delaminating floor panel, a stained back wall, rust at the pipe fittings, black spots on the cabinet bottom and a musty smell when you open the doors.

Slow drips from a P-trap or a supply connection often run for months. By the time a puddle appears, the particleboard under the sink may be ruined.

  • Cabinet floors that are soft, swollen or flaking.
  • Stains or mildew on the back wall or the underside of the countertop.
  • Water marks on the floor near the toe kick.
  • Rust or green corrosion on supply lines and fittings.

What to do right now.

Close the two angle stop valves under the sink, or the main water valve if they will not turn. Place a bucket under any drip and remove everything from the cabinet. Wipe up standing water and run a fan on the open cabinet.

Photograph the cabinet, the pipes and any wet flooring before cleanup. Do not use heat guns or space heaters in a closed cabinet, because wet materials near heat can warp or become a fire risk.

Cabinets, toe kicks and countertops.

Cabinet boxes are usually particleboard or medium-density fiberboard covered with a thin finish. Once those boards soak, they swell and do not return to shape. Solid wood cabinets and plywood boxes dry better, but finish damage may remain.

Water also runs behind toe kicks and into the subfloor under cabinets, where it cannot be seen. The crew removes the toe kick, drills small access holes if needed and dries the cavity with injection equipment. If the cabinet base is ruined, a replacement of that cabinet may be more practical than drying.

Flooring under and around the kitchen.

Kitchens are floored with vinyl, tile, laminate or hardwood, and each reacts to water differently. Vinyl plank and tile stay put but water can hide beneath them. Laminate swells at the seams. Wood floors cup as they absorb moisture.

See the hardwood floor page if your kitchen floor is wood. For water that reached the wall behind the cabinets, the wall and drywall page explains repair.

How the drying works.

The crew maps moisture in the cabinets, walls and floor, removes materials that cannot be dried, and sets air movers and a dehumidifier. Injection drying can reach wall cavities and the floor beneath cabinets without tearing out finished materials.

The kitchen is the most used room, so drying plans try to keep the sink and appliances available when possible. Ask the crew whether temporary plumbing connections are needed.

Insurance and kitchen leaks.

A sudden pipe failure or supply line break under a sink is often covered, while a drip that went unnoticed for months often is not. Insurers may ask whether the leak was gradual, so document when you first saw damage.

Keep the failed part if possible, and ask your insurer whether cabinets and countertops are handled as part of the dwelling claim.

Older kitchens in Machesney Park homes.

The village plan shows that more than 58 percent of homes were built before 1980, so many kitchens have decades-old cabinets and supply lines. Old shutoff valves can seize, and cabinet bases that were already stained are easy to miss.

Replacing old supply lines with braided stainless connectors and testing each shutoff once a year are cheap protections. If water has reached a basement below the kitchen, the basement page explains that cleanup.

Standards and sources behind this page.

Cabinet and subfloor drying follows ANSI/IICRC S500, which calls for moisture readings and documented drying goals. EPA guidance says to dry water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to help prevent mold.
