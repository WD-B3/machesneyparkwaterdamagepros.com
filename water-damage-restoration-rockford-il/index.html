Spring, summer and winter water in Rockford.

Rockford sees water damage in every season. Spring rain fills basements and creeks. Summer storms can drop heavy rain in an hour. Winter brings frozen pipes and ice dams, and thaws bring leaks that were hidden.

See frozen pipe water damage for winter plumbing failures and storm water damage cleanup for wind and rain.

Creek floods that shaped the city.

In 2006 and 2007, Keith Creek had 100-year flooding that damaged hundreds of older homes on the near east side. The city later used FEMA grants to demolish more than 100 homes and rebuilt the creek channel through 2019.

In July 2024, a 200-year flooding event overwhelmed the city stormwater systems. These events show that flooding in Rockford is not limited to the Rock River.

Waterways around Rockford.

The Rock River runs through the center of the city. The Kishwaukee River joins it on the south side, and Spring Creek, Keith Creek and Kent Creek also feed it. The Fordham Dam sits south of downtown.

Homes near those waterways face higher flood risk. See flood damage cleanup and basement water damage cleanup for how those losses are handled.

Older neighborhoods and historic buildings.

Rockford has a large inventory of older homes and historic buildings, including the Coronado Theatre from 1927 and the Faust Hotel from 1929. Older buildings often have plaster, masonry and older plumbing.

Drying them takes care. Read about structural drying and dehumidification for the process.

Commercial and multi-unit losses.

Rockford has many businesses and apartment buildings. Water that starts on one floor can travel through ceilings and walls to others, so quick action matters.

See commercial water damage restoration for how those jobs are handled.
