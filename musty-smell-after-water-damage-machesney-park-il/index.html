How to Get Rid of the Musty Smell After Water Damage in Machesney Park, IL.

A musty smell means something is still damp. Find it and dry it.

By the Machesney Park Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-11-10

☎ Call (815) 314-9281.

A musty smell after water damage is a sign that moisture remains somewhere. Air fresheners cover it for a day, but the smell returns until the source is found and dried. This guide shows Machesney Park, IL homeowners how to track the odor to its cause.

The steps are safe for most homes. If the smell is strong or comes with visible mold, plan for professional help.

What causes the smell.

Musty odor comes from microbial growth on damp materials and from the byproducts they release. Wet carpet pad, drywall, wood framing, insulation and cardboard can all hold moisture long after the surface feels dry.

Drying quickly, within about 24 to 48 hours, limits growth and the smell that comes with it.

How to find the source.

Follow your nose. The smell is stronger near the source. Open closets, check under sinks, lift corner carpet, look behind furniture on exterior walls and check the basement and crawl space.

A moisture meter confirms what you suspect. Thermal cameras show cool wet areas behind finishes.

Hidden spots that hold odor.

Carpet pad, the space under flooring, wall cavities, insulation and the inside of cabinets keep moisture the longest. HVAC ducts and drain pans can also hold damp air.

The carpet page and crawl space page cover two common hiding places.

Steps that actually work.

Remove what cannot be dried, dry what can, and clean what remains.

  1. Stop the water source.
  2. Remove wet porous materials like pad or soaked insulation.
  3. Run air movers and a dehumidifier until readings are normal.
  4. Clean hard surfaces with detergent and water.
  5. Recheck the smell after several days.

What does not work.

Sprays, candles and ozone shortcuts mask or partly treat the odor but do not remove wet material. Painting over a damp wall traps the smell inside.

If an odor returns after a few days, moisture is still present.

When the smell means mold.

A strong musty smell with visible growth, stained drywall or a recent leak suggests mold. Areas under about 10 square feet can be cleaned following EPA steps. Larger areas need professional help.

See the mold remediation page for containment and removal.

Why odors linger in porous materials.

Drywall, carpet pad, wood and insulation absorb water and odor molecules. Even after the surface feels dry, the inside may stay damp. That is why a smell can return on humid days after a flood seems finished.

Dehumidifiers pull the moisture from the air, and air movers carry moist air away from surfaces, so both are needed.

Using a dehumidifier correctly.

Close windows and doors in the room, place the dehumidifier near the center and empty or drain it regularly. Run it until humidity stays comfortably low and the smell fades. Clean the filter so it works at full strength.

A hygrometer, a cheap humidity gauge, helps you see whether the room is staying dry.

A room by room search.

Start where the water was, then widen the search. Check the carpet edge by pulling back a corner, look inside vanity cabinets, feel the base of walls, and check the back of closets. In a basement, look behind stored boxes and at the bottom of stairs.

Smell each spot up close. The strongest odor points to the wettest material.

When to replace instead of clean.

Carpet pad that stayed wet, soaked insulation and swollen particleboard rarely recover. Replacing them is cheaper than months of odor. Hard surfaces like tile, concrete and sealed wood usually clean up well.

Ask a crew to check moisture before deciding. Readings beat guesses.

Odor after a sewage or river water loss.

Water from sewage or river flooding is contaminated, and odors from it call for removal of affected porous materials, not just drying. Deodorizing alone does not make such a loss safe.

If your loss involved sewage, see the sewage backup page and keep people and pets away until the cleanup is done.

Tracking your progress.

Note the date, the humidity reading and how strong the smell is on a scale of one to five. If it fades day by day, drying is working. If it holds or grows, something is still wet.

A week of notes is often enough to know whether to keep going or call for help.

Machesney Park note: damp seasons.

The Village of Machesney Park 2040 Comprehensive Plan reports that more than 58 percent of village homes were built before 1980 and that a significant portion of the village lies in the 100-year floodplain of the Rock River.

Spring thaw and heavy rain can raise basement humidity in older homes. A dehumidifier and a working sump pump help keep smells from starting.

Prevent the smell from coming back.

Keep humidity in check, vent bathrooms and kitchens, fix leaks fast and dry any spill or flood within a day or two.
