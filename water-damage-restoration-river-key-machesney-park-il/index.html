Water damage restoration in River Key, Machesney Park, begins with finding the source and stopping it. River Key is a Rock River subdivision, and homes here face the same mix of plumbing failures and storm water as the rest of the village.

This page is for owners and renters in River Key and the streets around Key West Drive, Summerland Key and Sandy Point Drive. Listings place these addresses in ZIP code 61103, so call with your street address and the dispatcher will confirm coverage.

About the River Key subdivision.

The Village of Machesney Park 2040 Comprehensive Land Use Plan, adopted in August 2024, refers to the area as River Keys, while listings call it River Key. Property listings describe it as a Rock River subdivision with lots on Key West Drive, Summerland Key and Sandy Point Drive. Plat numbers such as River Key No. 3 show it was developed in phases.

Listings for some lots mention Rock River frontage, boat storage, city water and sewer, and sites where a walk-out basement may be possible. That is marketing language for individual lots. It does not tell you how your home was built, so check your own foundation type.

Floodplain information for River Key.

A real estate listing for one River Key lot says the lot is in a floodplain. That is a single listing, not a FEMA determination. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status for River Key was not verified for this page.

The Village plan says a significant portion of the village is in the 100-year floodplain of the Rock River. Use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center to check the exact address, and ask your insurer whether flood coverage applies to your home.

Water problems that suit newer riverfront homes.

Newer homes often have finished basements, upper-floor laundry rooms and plenty of appliances. A washing machine hose, a dishwasher line or a water heater can fail without a warning. See appliance leak water damage and water heater leak cleanup for how these losses are handled.

Near a river, groundwater can also push against a basement wall and slab. A sump pump that fails during a storm is a common reason for a wet lower level. The basement water damage cleanup page explains what happens next.

  • Turn off the water to a failed appliance at its valve if it is safe.
  • Check your sump pump and its outlet before heavy rain.
  • Know where your main water shutoff is.
  • Keep items off the floor in lower levels.

What drying looks like in a finished basement or lower level.

Finished lower levels often hide wet insulation, framing and subfloor behind drywall. The crew pumps standing water first, then measures moisture and removes what cannot be dried in place, such as saturated carpet pad. Air movers and dehumidifiers then run until readings return to a dry standard.

Read more on water extraction and structural drying and dehumidification. Photos and moisture logs also help when you talk to your insurer.

Insurance in River Key.

Homeowners policies generally cover sudden water damage from plumbing or appliance failures, and generally exclude flood unless a separate flood policy exists. Policies differ on sump and sewer backup, so ask before you pay a deductible. Your insurer decides coverage.

The restoration crew documents the damage and the work. It does not decide claims or guarantee payment.

Before the crew arrives in River Key.

These steps are general and aim to keep people safe first. If anything feels unsafe, stay out and call.

1Stop the source if it is safe.

Close the nearest shutoff valve or the main water valve. If the water came from outside, do not wade into it.

2Keep clear of electrics.

Do not touch outlets, cords or the panel if water is near them. Ask your utility or an electrician about power before going back in.

3Photograph the damage.

Take photos and short videos of the water, the affected rooms and damaged belongings once it is safe to enter.

4Lift what you safely can.

Move small items, rugs and paperwork to dry ground. Leave heavy furniture and anything you cannot lift safely.
