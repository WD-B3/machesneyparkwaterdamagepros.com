What a Sudden Jump in Your Water Bill Can Mean in Machesney Park, IL.

A higher bill is often the first sign of a leak you cannot see. A meter test takes ten minutes.

By the Machesney Park Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-11-10

☎ Call (815) 314-9281.

When a water bill jumps and nothing has changed in the household, a leak is a likely cause. Leaks can run for weeks before anyone sees a stain. This guide shows Machesney Park, IL homeowners how to check the meter, find common culprits and decide when to call for help.

The steps below are safe for most homes and take little time.

Check whether the meter moves.

Turn off every water-using appliance and fixture in the home. Find your water meter, often in the basement or in a pit near the street, and read it. Wait fifteen minutes without using water and read it again.

If the number changed, or a small leak indicator is spinning, water is leaking somewhere on your side of the meter.

Common causes of a higher bill.

Toilets are the most frequent culprit. A flapper that does not seal lets water run into the bowl all day. Dripping faucets, a stuck irrigation valve, a softener that cycles too often and a leaking water heater are others.

  • A toilet that runs or refills on its own.
  • A dripping faucet or showerhead.
  • Irrigation or hose bib that leaks.
  • Water heater connections.
  • A buried line between meter and house.

The toilet dye test.

Add a few drops of food coloring to the toilet tank and wait fifteen minutes without flushing. If color appears in the bowl, the flapper leaks. Replacing a flapper is cheap and fast.

Check every toilet, including guest baths.

Hidden leaks in walls, floors and slabs.

If fixtures are fine but the meter still moves, the leak may be inside a wall, under a floor or in a buried line. Warm spots on a floor, damp carpet, sounds of running water and sudden low pressure are clues.

The slab leak page and burst pipe page explain those cases.

Seasonal and usage changes to rule out.

Before assuming a leak, rule out normal changes. Guests, a new pool or garden watering, filling a tank, or a billing cycle with more days than usual can raise a bill.

Compare the usage number, not just the dollar amount, with last year's same month.

What to do if you find a leak.

Shut off the water to the fixture or the main valve. Photograph the damage. If water has soaked materials, dry them within 24 to 48 hours. A plumber repairs the pipe and a restoration crew dries the structure.

Ask your water provider whether it offers any review of unusually high bills, and ask your insurer about coverage.

Reading your bill like a detective.

Look at the usage number, not just the dollar total. Compare it with the same month last year and with the previous two bills. Check the billing dates, because a longer billing period raises usage without any leak.

If the increase is steep and steady, a continuous leak is likely. If it spikes once, a one time event such as filling a pool or a stuck valve is more likely.

Finding a leak you cannot see.

Walk the yard and look for soggy spots, unusually green grass or sinking soil along the line from the meter to the house. Listen at the meter and at the main valve for a faint hiss while all water is off.

A plumber can use acoustic and thermal tools to trace a buried line. Restoration crews use moisture meters and thermal cameras to find wet materials inside the structure.

Keeping a simple usage log.

Read your meter at the same time each week and write the number down. A steady rise with no change in habits points to a leak. A log also helps a provider or plumber see when a problem began.

Take a photo of the meter each time so you can show the readings later.

Machesney Park note: ask your water provider.

Water and sewer arrangements vary by address in this part of Winnebago County. Ask your water provider how to read your meter, what the billing period covers and whether it reviews unusually high bills.

The Village of Machesney Park Public Works Department can point you to the right provider for your address.

When to call a professional.

Call if the meter moves with all fixtures off, if you find wet drywall or floors, or if you hear water in the walls. Thermal imaging and moisture meters can trace the wet area.
