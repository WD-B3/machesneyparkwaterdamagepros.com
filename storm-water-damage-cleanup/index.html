Wind and rain can open a building to water. Limit the damage first, then dry.

Storm water damage cleanup in Machesney Park, IL covers water that enters through roofs, windows, siding and doors during wind, hail and heavy rain. Cover openings if it is safe, move belongings away from water and call the 24/7 line.

Rising water from a river is a different case, covered on the flood damage page.

Storm emergency steps.

Stay safe first. Do not climb onto a damaged roof or go near downed power lines. If you can reach an opening from inside, cover it with plastic sheeting or a tarp, and photograph the damage before you move things.

Call your insurer and a roofer for emergency tarping. A roofer limits more rain from entering, and restoration handles water that is already inside.

  • Stay clear of downed lines and standing water near them.
  • Cover openings only if it is safe to reach them.
  • Move belongings away from leaks.
  • Photograph damage before cleanup.

How storms let water in.

Wind lifts shingles, hail dents flashing, and rain finds the gaps. Water enters attics and runs down walls and ceilings. Windows and doors can leak in wind-driven rain, and siding can fail at seams.

The result is often hidden inside walls and insulation, so moisture mapping matters even when the visible damage looks small.

Cleanup and drying after a storm.

The crew extracts water, removes wet insulation and drywall, and dries the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers. A roofer repairs the building envelope, and a contractor rebuilds what was removed.

The ceiling water damage page covers ceiling-specific repair, and structural drying covers the drying itself.

Weather in the Rockford area.

NWS climate normals for Rockford, 1991 to 2020, show 37.24 inches of precipitation and 37.1 inches of snowfall a year. That means rain, snowmelt and ice all challenge roofs and drainage in this area.

These normals are measured at the Rockford station, not at each house in the village, but they show that a mix of rain and snow is a normal part of the year.

Ice dams, snowmelt and winter storm water.

Not every storm leak comes from rain. In winter, heat escaping into an attic melts snow on the roof, and the water refreezes at the eaves where the roof is colder. The ice forms a dam, and meltwater backs up under shingles and runs into the attic and down interior walls.

With roughly 37 inches of snow a year at the Rockford station, according to NWS normals, northern Illinois roofs deal with this kind of cycle regularly. Better attic insulation and ventilation reduce ice dam risk, but once water is inside, the cleanup is the same as for any roof leak.

  • Do not chip ice off the roof yourself, since shingles and gutters are easy to damage.
  • Look for stains on ceilings along exterior walls.
  • Check attic insulation for dampness after a thaw.
  • Call a roofer to relieve the dam and restoration to dry what got wet.

Documenting a storm loss for your claim.

Take photographs of the roof, siding and windows from the ground, and of every wet interior surface. Note the date and time of the storm and when you first saw water. If a roofer tarps the roof, keep the invoice and photographs of the damage they found.

Your insurer may send an adjuster, and may want to see damaged materials before they are discarded. Ask before throwing anything out, and ask the restoration crew to record moisture readings so the interior scope matches the exterior damage.

Insurance and storms.

Wind and hail damage are often covered by homeowners insurance, and flood damage from rising water is not. Ask about any separate wind or hail deductible, and ask whether temporary repairs are reimbursed.

File promptly and keep receipts for emergency repairs. Photograph the roof damage and the inside damage together so the connection is clear.
